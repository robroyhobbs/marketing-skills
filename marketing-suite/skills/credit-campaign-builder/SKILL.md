# Credit Campaign Builder

Stop hoarding your credits. Start giving them away.

This skill designs incentive campaigns where compute/usage becomes your marketing budget, not your COGS.

---

## Core Philosophy

**Marketing as Compute > Marketing as Ads**

**The Lovable Insight:**

Traditional SaaS: LLM costs are COGS (Cost of Goods Sold). Minimize them.

AI-native approach: LLM costs are CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost). Invest in them strategically.

**The math:**
- Google Ads: $50 to acquire one customer who might not convert
- Vibe Grant: $50 of credits to acquire one customer who's already using your product

**Which is better?**

The customer who burned $50 of your credits building something cool has:
1. Experienced the product deeply
2. Created something they're invested in
3. A reason to share what they built
4. Social proof from their accomplishment

The customer from Google Ads clicked a link and might bounce.

**This skill designs campaigns where giving away usage drives better acquisition than buying ads.**

## The Marketing as Compute Framework

### Traditional vs AI-Native Economics

| Factor | Traditional SaaS | AI-Native (Credit Campaigns) |
|--------|------------------|------------------------------|
| **Free tier** | Limited features, unlimited time | Unlimited features, limited compute |
| **Acquisition cost** | Paid ads ($50-200 per customer) | Credits given ($20-100 in compute) |
| **Conversion proof** | User clicked ad, visited page | User built something, invested time |
| **Sharing incentive** | None (they get free product) | High (they created something to show off) |
| **Viral coefficient** | Low (~0.3-0.5) | High (~1.2-2.0 with proper structure) |
| **Attribution** | Hard (multi-touch) | Clear (credit grant → build → share → signup) |

**Key insight:** A user who consumes $50 of your compute to build something is worth more than a user who saw your $50 ad.

Why?
- They've experienced product value firsthand
- They're emotionally invested (sunk cost)
- They have something to show off (viral artifact)
- They've self-selected as high-intent

**Result:** Lower CAC, higher LTV, built-in virality.

## The Five Campaign Types

### Type 1: The "Vibe Grant"

**What it is:** Unlimited usage for a limited time, given to specific user segments.

**Best for:** Creative tools, AI apps, no-code builders, content generators.

**Structure:**
- Grant duration: 24-72 hours
- Usage: Unlimited (or very high limit like $500 in compute)
- Qualification: "Build in public" requirement
- Viral loop: Must share creation to keep access beyond initial grant

**Example: Lovable's approach**
- Give unlimited usage for 48 hours
- Requirement: Share your build journey on X/LinkedIn
- Result: Every grant = potential viral post + real user validation

**When to use:**
- Pre-launch (controlled beta)
- Testing new features (power users only)
- Events (hackathons, launch weeks)
- Influencer seeding (give to creators with audiences)

**ROI calculation:**
- Cost per grant: $50-200 (compute burned)
- Shares per grant: 30-60% share rate
- Signups per shared build: 5-15 signups
- Effective CAC: $3-40 per signup (vs $50-200 for ads)

---

### Type 2: The "Build Weekend Challenge"

**What it is:** Time-bound competition where participants build with unlimited credits, winners get featured/rewarded.

**Best for:** Developer tools, AI platforms, creative software, productivity apps.

**Structure:**
- Duration: 48-72 hours (Friday evening → Sunday night)
- Challenge: "Build [specific thing] using [your product]"
- Credits: Unlimited during challenge window
- Prizes: Top 10 get extended free access, #1 gets featured + cash/swag
- Requirement: Public submission (showcase page)

**Example mechanics:**
- Friday 6pm: Challenge announced, credits activated
- Saturday/Sunday: Build time, live leaderboard
- Monday: Judging, winners announced
- Ongoing: Gallery of all submissions (permanent marketing asset)

**Viral loops:**
- Participants share their progress (build-in-public posts)
- Submissions live on public gallery (SEO + social proof)
- Winners get featured (status incentive)
- Non-winners want to try again next month

**When to use:**
- Monthly recurring event (builds habit)
- Product launch (showcase capabilities)
- Feature release (demonstrate new functionality)
- Community building (regular participants become evangelists)

**ROI calculation:**
- Cost per challenge: $2,000-10,000 (compute for 50-200 participants)
- Participants: 50-200 (depends on audience size)
- Public builds created: 50-200 (permanent marketing assets)
- Signups from challenge: 100-500 (viewers who didn't participate)
- Effective CAC: $4-50 per signup

---

### Type 3: The "Referral Credits" Program

**What it is:** Give credits to both referrer and referee when new user signs up.

**Best for:** Any product with consumable credits, especially collaboration tools.

**Structure:**
- Referrer reward: $20-50 in credits when referee signs up
- Referee reward: $20-50 in credits on signup
- Bonus tier: Referrer gets extra $100 when referee converts to paid
- Public leaderboard: Top referrers get status + additional rewards

**Example tiers:**
```
Bronze Referrer (1-5 signups): $25 per referral
Silver Referrer (6-20 signups): $35 per referral + "Partner" badge
Gold Referrer (21-50 signups): $50 per referral + featured in newsletter
Platinum Referrer (51+ signups): Custom partnership terms
```

**Viral loops:**
- Users want more credits → invite others
- New users get credits immediately → try product → invite others
- Top referrers get status → share leaderboard position → more signups

**When to use:**
- Ongoing (always-on growth lever)
- Supplement paid ads (better LTV customers)
- Community-building (rewards advocates)
- Scaling proven PMF (double down on organic)

**ROI calculation:**
- Cost per referral pair: $40-100 (credits to both parties)
- Conversion rate: 30-50% of referred users become active
- Active users who refer: 10-20%
- Viral coefficient: 1.0-1.5 (self-sustaining to growing)
- Effective CAC: $40-100 (but high-intent, referred customers)

---

### Type 4: The "Underserved Audience Grant"

**What it is:** Free credits for specific demographic groups that are underrepresented in your user base.

**Best for:** Products wanting to build diverse communities and reach new markets.

**Structure:**
- Target audience: Women in tech, non-technical founders, students, non-profits, etc.
- Grant amount: 3-6 months of free usage
- Qualification: Application process (verify identity/affiliation)
- Requirement: Optional but encouraged: Share your use case publicly

**Example: "She Builds" Program (Lovable's approach)**
- Target: Women builders (designers, PMs, founders)
- Grant: 6 months unlimited usage
- Community: Private Slack for She Builds members
- Showcase: Monthly highlight of best builds

**Viral loops:**
- Underserved audience feels seen (emotional connection)
- Community forms around program (network effects)
- Members share with similar demographics (targeted virality)
- Media coverage (PR value)

**When to use:**
- Expanding into new markets (students, international, etc.)
- Building community (shared identity)
- PR/brand building (social impact angle)
- Competitive differentiation (competitors don't serve this segment)

**ROI calculation:**
- Cost per grant: $200-1,000 (6 months free)
- Grants issued: 100-500 (depends on budget)
- Media value: $10,000-100,000 (PR coverage)
- Community value: Priceless (evangelists for life)
- Effective CAC: $200-1,000 initially, but high LTV + referrals

---

### Type 5: The "Creator Fund"

**What it is:** Pay creators (in credits or cash) to build publicly using your product.

**Best for:** Products needing content, tutorials, or use case demonstrations.

**Structure:**
- Selected creators: 10-50 (depends on budget)
- Compensation: $500-5,000 per creator (credits + cash)
- Deliverable: Build something, document the process, share publicly
- Content rights: Creator retains ownership, you get usage rights
- Duration: 1-3 months

**Example mechanics:**
- Application: Creators apply with proposal (what they'll build)
- Selection: Choose based on audience fit + content quality
- Building: Creators build, share progress publicly
- Showcase: Final builds featured on your site
- Amplification: You share their content to your audience

**Viral loops:**
- Creator shares to their audience (paid distribution)
- Audience sees use case (social proof + education)
- Some audience members sign up
- Their builds become more content (flywheel)

**When to use:**
- Content marketing (tutorials, showcases)
- Entering new markets (vertical-specific creators)
- Product education (show advanced use cases)
- SEO (creator content ranks for long-tail keywords)

**ROI calculation:**
- Cost per creator: $500-5,000
- Creators: 10-50
- Total cost: $5,000-250,000
- Content pieces created: 50-500 (tutorials, showcases, social posts)
- Reach: 100,000-5,000,000 (depends on creator audiences)
- Signups: 500-10,000
- Effective CAC: $5-500 (wide range, depends on creator quality)

## The Campaign Design Process

### Step 1: Calculate Your Credit Economics

Before designing any campaign, understand your unit economics.

**Questions to answer:**
1. **What's your cost per compute unit?**
   - Example: $0.01 per AI agent task, $0.05 per image generation, $0.10 per video render

2. **What's your average usage per active user?**
   - Example: 50 tasks/month = $0.50 in compute costs

3. **What's your current CAC (via paid ads)?**
   - Example: $100 per signup from Google Ads

4. **What's your conversion rate (free → paid)?**
   - Example: 10% of free users convert to paid within 3 months

5. **What's your target LTV?**
   - Example: $500 average LTV over 12 months

**The credit campaign threshold:**

If giving away $X in credits attracts a user with higher intent than a $X paid ad, credit campaigns win.

**Example calculation:**
- Paid ad CAC: $100, conversion rate: 5%, cost per converted customer: $2,000
- Credit grant: $50, conversion rate: 20%, cost per converted customer: $250

Credit campaigns are 8x more efficient in this scenario.

**Decision rule:**
Run credit campaigns when: (Credit cost × Intent uplift) < Paid CAC

---

### Step 2: Choose Campaign Type Based on Goals

| Goal | Best Campaign Type | Why |
|------|-------------------|-----|
| **Rapid user acquisition** | Vibe Grant | Fastest to implement, broadest appeal |
| **Build community** | Underserved Audience Grant | Creates emotional connection + network effects |
| **Content creation** | Creator Fund | Generates educational content + use cases |
| **Ongoing growth** | Referral Credits | Always-on, compounds over time |
| **Showcase capabilities** | Build Weekend Challenge | Concentrates attention, creates portfolio |
| **Feature adoption** | Limited-Time Credits | Incentivizes trying new functionality |

**Pro tip:** Stack campaign types.

Example:
- Base layer: Referral credits (always on)
- Monthly: Build Weekend Challenge
- Quarterly: Underserved Audience Grant cohort
- Annually: Creator Fund (10 selected creators)

Stacking creates year-round activity + multiple touchpoints.

---

### Step 3: Design Qualification Criteria

**The tension:** Broad qualification = more users. Strict qualification = higher intent.

**Qualification spectrum:**

**No qualification (open to all):**
- Pro: Maximum participation
- Con: Low-intent users, potential abuse
- Use when: You want volume + can handle noise

**Light qualification (email signup):**
- Pro: Easy to implement, filters bots
- Con: Still gets tire-kickers
- Use when: Standard approach for most campaigns

**Medium qualification (application form):**
- Pro: Filters for intent, collects user data
- Con: Reduces participation by 50-70%
- Use when: Limited budget, want high-quality users

**Heavy qualification (interview + portfolio):**
- Pro: Only best users, strong community
- Con: Reduces participation by 90%+
- Use when: Creator Fund, Underserved Audience Grant

**Smart qualification tactics:**

1. **Progressive qualification:**
   - Start easy (email signup)
   - Unlock more credits with actions (complete tutorial, invite friend, share build)

2. **Reputation-based:**
   - New users: $20 credits
   - Users with public GitHub/portfolio: $100 credits
   - Users with referrals from existing members: $200 credits

3. **Time-based:**
   - First 100 signups: Unlimited credits
   - Next 500: $100 credits
   - After 600: $20 credits
   - Creates urgency

---

### Step 4: Build Viral Loops

**Critical:** Credit campaigns must have built-in distribution, or they're just expensive free trials.

**The viral loop formula:**

```
User receives credits
  ↓
User builds something
  ↓
User shares creation (incentivized or required)
  ↓
Viewers see creation + product
  ↓
Viewers sign up
  ↓
New users receive credits
  ↓
Cycle repeats
```

**Mechanisms to enforce/incentivize sharing:**

**1. Required sharing (hard loop):**
- "Share your build to Twitter/LinkedIn to unlock full credits"
- "Post must include @YourProduct mention + #YourHashtag"
- System verifies post exists before unlocking

**2. Incentivized sharing (soft loop):**
- Base grant: $50 credits
- Bonus: Share your build → +$50 credits
- Bonus: Get 5 upvotes on your build → +$50 credits
- Bonus: Your build gets featured → +$200 credits

**3. Public by default:**
- All builds go to public gallery (unless user opts out)
- Gallery is SEO-optimized (each build = landing page)
- Users can see "Built with [YourProduct]" attribution
- One-click "Remix this" button

**4. Leaderboard mechanics:**
- Public leaderboard of top builders
- Metrics: Credits consumed, builds created, community upvotes
- Top 10 get "Power Builder" badge
- Status > money for many users

**5. Collaboration requirements:**
- "Invite a teammate to get +$100 credits"
- Collaborative builds get featured more prominently
- Team challenges (teams of 2-4 build together)

**Viral coefficient targets:**

- 0.5-0.8: Campaign is not self-sustaining but supplements paid
- 0.8-1.0: Break-even, campaign sustains itself
- 1.0-1.5: Strong viral growth, scale aggressively
- 1.5+: Exceptional, pour fuel on fire

---

### Step 5: Set Success Metrics

**Primary metrics:**

1. **Viral coefficient (K)**
   - Formula: (Credits given per user) × (Signups per credit recipient)
   - Target: K > 1.0 for self-sustaining campaigns

2. **Effective CAC**
   - Formula: (Total credits given) / (New signups)
   - Target: < Your paid ad CAC

3. **Conversion rate (free → paid)**
   - Formula: (Credit users who convert to paid) / (Total credit users)
   - Target: 2-5x higher than standard free trial conversion

4. **Share rate**
   - Formula: (Users who share builds) / (Users who receive credits)
   - Target: >30% for optional sharing, 80%+ for required sharing

**Secondary metrics:**

5. **Time to activation**
   - How long until credit user completes first meaningful action?
   - Target: <24 hours (faster = higher intent)

6. **Credit consumption rate**
   - What % of granted credits are actually used?
   - Target: >60% (if low, credits aren't valuable enough)

7. **Builds created per user**
   - How many projects/artifacts do users create?
   - Target: >1 (repeat usage = product stickiness)

8. **Content value**
   - How many builds end up in public gallery?
   - SEO value (do gallery pages rank)?
   - Social shares (how viral are the builds)?

**Dashboard tracking:**

Track weekly cohorts:
```
Week 1 (Jan 1-7):
- Credits distributed: $10,000
- Recipients: 200
- Builds created: 145
- Public shares: 87
- Signups from shares: 43
- Viral coefficient: 0.22 (need improvement)
- Effective CAC: $232 (worse than ads, iterate)

Week 2 (Jan 8-14):
- Credits distributed: $10,000
- Recipients: 200
- Builds created: 189 (improved with better onboarding)
- Public shares: 151 (added incentives)
- Signups from shares: 227
- Viral coefficient: 1.14 (self-sustaining!)
- Effective CAC: $44 (5x better than ads, scale up)
```

## Campaign Playbooks

### Playbook 1: Pre-Launch Vibe Grant (Generate Buzz)

**Goal:** Create social proof and user-generated content before public launch.

**Timeline:** 4-6 weeks before launch

**Structure:**
- Target: 50-100 selected beta users (hand-picked or applications)
- Grant: Unlimited usage until launch
- Requirement: Share at least one build publicly per week
- Bonus: Best builds get featured in launch materials

**Week-by-week:**

**Week 1-2 (Onboarding):**
- Send beta invites with personal notes
- Activate unlimited credits
- Private Slack channel for beta users
- Daily check-ins: "What are you building today?"

**Week 3-4 (Building):**
- Users build, you amplify their shares
- Weekly showcase: "Best builds of the week"
- Mid-campaign bonus: Extra $200 credits to top 10 most active

**Week 5-6 (Launch Prep):**
- Collect testimonials from beta users
- Create case studies from best builds
- Compile gallery of all builds for launch page
- Prepare beta users to share on launch day

**Launch Day:**
- Beta users share their builds + launch announcement
- 50-100 shares hit social media simultaneously
- Looks like organic groundswell (because it is!)

**ROI:**
- Cost: $5,000-15,000 (compute for 50-100 power users)
- Social proof: 50-100 real builds created before launch
- Launch amplification: 50-100 people sharing on day 1
- Effective: Better than spending $15K on launch ads

---

### Playbook 2: Monthly Build Challenge (Recurring Growth)

**Goal:** Predictable monthly spike in signups + content creation.

**Timeline:** First weekend of every month

**Structure:**
- Theme changes monthly (align with feature releases)
- Duration: 48 hours (Friday 6pm → Sunday 6pm)
- Credits: Unlimited during challenge
- Prizes: Top 10 get 3 months free, #1 gets $500 + feature

**Monthly cycle:**

**Week 1 (Challenge weekend):**
- Friday: Announce theme, open registration, activate credits
- Saturday/Sunday: Participants build, live leaderboard updates
- Sunday evening: Submissions close
- Monday: Judge, announce winners

**Week 2 (Showcase):**
- Blog post: "Top 10 builds from this month's challenge"
- Email newsletter: Showcase gallery
- Social: Share individual builds throughout week
- Winners share their prizes (status signaling)

**Week 3 (Analysis):**
- Analyze metrics: What worked, what didn't?
- Interview top builders: Why did you participate?
- Plan next month's theme
- Recruit judges for next month

**Week 4 (Promotion):**
- Tease next month's challenge
- Email last month's participants
- Social posts: Countdown to next challenge
- Retarget non-participants with winning builds

**Flywheel effect:**

Month 1: 50 participants, 30 public builds, 75 signups
Month 2: 75 participants (past participants return), 55 builds, 140 signups
Month 3: 120 participants (word spreads), 95 builds, 280 signups
Month 6: 300 participants (now a known event), 240 builds, 850 signups

**ROI per challenge:**
- Cost: $3,000-8,000 (compute for participants + prizes)
- Builds created: 50-300 (permanent content assets)
- Signups: 100-800 (depending on maturity)
- Effective CAC: $10-60

---

### Playbook 3: Referral Credits (Always-On Growth)

**Goal:** Turn every happy user into a distribution channel.

**Implementation:**

**1. Activation Flow:**
- New user signs up → Welcome email includes referral link
- Dashboard has prominent "Invite & Earn" section
- First login shows: "Get $25 credits for every friend who joins"

**2. Reward Structure:**
```
Referrer:
- Friend signs up: $25 credits
- Friend completes tutorial: +$25 credits
- Friend invites someone: +$10 credits (2nd degree)
- Friend converts to paid: $100 credits

Referee:
- Immediate: $25 credits on signup
- Complete tutorial: +$25 credits
- First build: +$50 credits
```

**3. Viral Mechanics:**
- Personal referral page: yourproduct.com/matt (vanity URL)
- Referral stats dashboard: "You've referred 14 people, earned $500"
- Leaderboard: Top referrers get featured + bonus rewards
- Milestones: 5 referrals = Bronze, 20 = Silver, 50 = Gold

**4. Amplification:**
- Weekly email: "You have $150 in pending credits! 3 friends pending signup"
- Monthly showcase: "Top referrer this month: Jane (42 referrals)"
- Auto-generated social posts: "I just earned $500 in [Product] credits by sharing with my team"

**ROI:**
- Cost per referral pair: $50-150 (credits to both parties)
- Participation rate: 15-25% of users refer at least one person
- Viral coefficient: 0.3-0.6 per user (0.6 = 60% of users refer >1 person)
- Sustainable growth: 20-40% of signups come from referrals

### Playbook 4: The "She Builds" Grant (Community + PR)

**Goal:** Build community in underserved segment + generate positive press.

**Timeline:** Quarterly cohorts (accept 50-100 per quarter)

**Structure:**
- Target: Women builders (founders, designers, developers, PMs)
- Grant: 6 months unlimited usage
- Application: Short form (name, what you'll build, social links)
- Community: Private Slack for She Builds members

**Quarter-by-quarter:**

**Q1 (Launch):**
- Announce program via social + email
- Accept first 100 applicants
- Create private Slack channel
- Weekly "Show & Tell" calls
- End of quarter: Showcase top 10 builds

**Q2 (Iterate):**
- Second cohort: 100 more members
- Add mentorship component (alumni mentor new members)
- First "She Builds Summit" (virtual event)
- PR push: Tech publications cover program

**Q3 (Scale):**
- Third cohort: 150 members
- Corporate partnerships (companies sponsor members)
- IRL meetups in 3-5 cities
- Gallery of 500+ builds from She Builds members

**Q4 (Institutionalize):**
- Program is now core to brand identity
- 500+ active She Builds members
- Dedicated community manager
- Annual summit with sponsors

**ROI:**
- Cost per member: $500-1,000 (6 months free)
- Members per cohort: 50-150
- Total cost: $25,000-150,000 per year
- PR value: $50,000-500,000 (media coverage)
- Community value: Immeasurable (lifelong advocates)
- Effective CAC: High initially, but retention + LTV + referrals make it worth it

**Note:** This isn't about short-term ROI. It's about building a moat.

## Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 1: No Viral Loop

**The mistake:** Give away credits, hope users share, no enforcement.

**Result:** 5% share rate, campaign doesn't scale.

**Fix:** Require sharing OR incentivize heavily (see viral loop section).

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 2: Generic Credits

**The mistake:** "$50 credit" with no context or guidance.

**Result:** Users don't know what $50 gets them, don't use it.

**Fix:** Frame credits in terms of outcomes.
- Bad: "$50 in credits"
- Good: "Enough credits to generate 100 images" or "Build 3 full apps"

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 3: No Time Limit

**The mistake:** "Here are unlimited credits forever."

**Result:** No urgency, users don't activate.

**Fix:** Time-bound grants create urgency.
- "48 hours of unlimited usage"
- "$500 credits, expires in 30 days"

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 4: Hidden Referral Program

**The mistake:** Referral program exists but isn't promoted.

**Result:** 2-5% of users ever refer someone.

**Fix:** Make referrals impossible to miss.
- Dashboard banner
- Email reminders
- Exit intent popup: "Before you go, invite a friend for $25 credits"

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 5: Ignoring Abuse

**The mistake:** No fraud detection, users create fake accounts for credits.

**Result:** Budget explodes, 30-50% of "users" are bots/abuse.

**Fix:** Basic fraud prevention.
- Email verification required
- Rate limiting on credit grants
- Manual review for high-value grants
- Blacklist disposable email domains
- Require social account connection for large grants

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 6: Treating Credits Like Discounts

**The mistake:** "$50 off your first month!" instead of "$50 to build."

**Result:** Discount shoppers, not product builders.

**Fix:** Frame credits as empowerment, not savings.
- Bad: "50% off"
- Good: "Build your first app free"

## Reference: The Credit Campaign Maturity Model

### Stage 1: Experiment (Month 1-3)

**Activities:**
- Run one small campaign (50-100 users, $5,000 budget)
- Test one campaign type (recommend: Vibe Grant or Build Challenge)
- Manual operations (you manage everything)
- Basic tracking (spreadsheet)

**Success criteria:**
- Viral coefficient >0.5
- Effective CAC < 2x paid ad CAC
- Learn what works

---

### Stage 2: Systematize (Month 4-9)

**Activities:**
- Run campaigns monthly (predictable cadence)
- Build automation (credit distribution, verification)
- Dashboard tracking (real-time metrics)
- Iterate based on data

**Success criteria:**
- Viral coefficient >0.8
- Effective CAC < paid ad CAC
- 20%+ of signups come from campaigns

---

### Stage 3: Scale (Month 10-18)

**Activities:**
- Multiple campaign types running simultaneously
- Referral program always-on
- Monthly challenges standard
- Quarterly community cohorts
- Annual creator fund

**Success criteria:**
- Viral coefficient >1.0 (self-sustaining)
- Effective CAC < 50% of paid ad CAC
- 50%+ of signups come from campaigns
- Credit campaigns are primary growth channel

---

### Stage 4: Institutionalize (Month 18+)

**Activities:**
- Credit campaigns are core go-to-market
- Paid ads are supplementary, not primary
- Community is brand moat
- User-generated content is primary marketing asset

**Success criteria:**
- Viral coefficient >1.2
- Effective CAC < 30% of paid ad CAC
- 70%+ of signups come from campaigns
- Product growth compounds organically

## Conclusion

Credit campaigns flip the script:

**Old way:** Hoard usage, monetize every action, free tiers are cheap.

**New way:** Give usage away strategically, users become distribution, free users are high-intent.

**The key insight:** In AI products, the marginal cost of compute is real but small compared to CAC.

If burning $50 of compute creates a power user who refers 3 friends, that's a better investment than a $50 ad click.

**This is marketing as compute.**

Build campaigns that turn your product usage into your marketing engine.
