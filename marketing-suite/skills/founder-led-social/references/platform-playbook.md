## Platform-Specific Best Practices

### Twitter/X Deep Dive

#### Algorithm Mechanics (2024-2025)

**What the algorithm rewards:**
1. **Engagement velocity**: First 30 minutes determine reach
2. **Reply ratio**: Replies > retweets > likes (conversation signals quality)
3. **Dwell time**: How long people spend reading your thread (longer = better)
4. **Video watch time**: Auto-play keeps users on platform (algorithm loves this)
5. **Quote tweets**: People adding commentary = high-quality signal
6. **Profile visits**: If people click to your profile = interested in you, not just tweet

**What the algorithm punishes:**
1. **External links in main tweet**: Kills reach (algorithm wants users to stay on X)
2. **Low engagement**: If first 100 people ignore it, algorithm stops showing it
3. **Copy-paste replies**: Spammy behavior detected
4. **Too many hashtags**: Looks spammy (1-2 max)

**Tactical optimization:**

**Do this:**
- Post thread → put link in REPLY to thread (not main tweet)
- Ask question at end of thread (drives replies)
- Reply to your own tweet 2-3 hours later with additional insight (second wave of reach)
- Quote-tweet yourself next day with "Adding one more thing..." (third wave)

**Don't do this:**
- Link in first tweet (dead on arrival)
- No visuals (scrollers keep scrolling)
- Posting same time every day without testing (audiences vary)

#### Content Formats That Perform

**1. The "Here's What I Learned" Thread**

**Structure:**
```
Tweet 1: I just [completed experience]. Here are the [number] things I learned.
Tweet 2-X: [Numbered insights, each specific and actionable]
Final tweet: [Summary + soft CTA]
```

**Why it works:** Educational value + numbered structure (completeness) + founder credibility.

**Example:**
```
I just analyzed 100 failed startups to see why they died.

Here are the 7 patterns:

1. Built product nobody wanted (42% of failures)
- Made tech first, customers second
- No validation before building
...

If you're pre-PMF, avoid these: [link to full analysis]
```

---

**2. The "Transparent Metrics" Post**

**Structure:**
```
[Milestone] metrics:
- Metric 1: [specific number]
- Metric 2: [specific number]
- Metric 3: [specific number]

What worked: [1-2 tactics]
What didn't: [1-2 failed experiments]
Next: [what you're focused on]
```

**Why it works:** Transparency builds trust + benchmarks help others + vulnerability (sharing failures).

**Example:**
```
October numbers:
- $89K MRR (+18% MoM)
- 412 customers (+12%)
- 91% net retention (-3% 😬)

What worked: Shipping daily (45 updates this month)
What didn't: Cold outbound (0.2% conversion, stopping)

November focus: Fix retention (exit interviews w/ churned customers)
```

---

**3. The "Here's How We Built It" Technical Thread**

**Structure:**
```
Tweet 1: We just shipped [feature]. Here's the full technical breakdown.
Tweets 2-5: The problem + constraints
Tweets 6-10: Approaches we tried + why they failed
Tweets 11-15: The solution + code snippets/architecture diagrams
Final tweets: Lessons + resources
```

**Why it works:** Technical depth proves expertise + educational for developers + shows product substance.

---

**4. The "Hot Take / Contrarian Opinion"**

**Structure:**
```
Tweet 1: [Bold claim that goes against conventional wisdom]

Here's why:

Tweets 2-5: Why conventional wisdom exists + seems logical
Tweets 6-9: Why it's actually wrong (evidence + examples)
Tweets 10-12: The alternative approach
Final tweet: Caveat + CTA
```

**Why it works:** Controversy drives engagement + challenges readers = they share to add their take.

**Example:**
```
Unpopular opinion: "Move fast and break things" is terrible advice.

Here's why:

1/ Everyone remembers the "move fast" part.
Nobody remembers "break things" was about their internal tools, not production.
...

If you're pre-PMF: Move fast.
If you have customers: Don't break their stuff.
```

**Structure:**

[Why it matters - 1-2 sentences]

[Link to try it]

**Why it works:** Video auto-plays in feed (attention) + shows > tells + algorithm favors video.

#### Engagement Tactics

**Tactic 1: The First-Hour Reply Blitz**

**What:** Reply to every comment in first hour after posting.

**Why:** Algorithm sees "high engagement" and pushes tweet to more people.

**How:**
- Set 30-minute timer after posting
- Reply with substance (not just "Thanks!"), ideally adds value or continues conversation
- Ask follow-up questions (drives more replies = more algorithm boost)

---

**Tactic 2: The Strategic Quote-Tweet**

**What:** Quote-tweet your own thread 2-3 hours later with additional insight.

**Why:** Second wave of impressions to people who missed first round.

**How:**
```
Original thread: [Your thread about feature launch]

2 hours later, quote-tweet with:
"One more thing I forgot to mention in the thread above:

[Additional insight, common mistake to avoid, or reader question you've answered in DMs]"
```

---

**Tactic 3: The "Reply Guy" (Strategic, Not Spammy)**

**What:** Reply to relevant tweets in your niche with genuinely helpful insights.

**Why:** Gets you in front of relevant audiences, builds reputation.

**How:**
- Follow 20-30 accounts in your space (competitors, adjacent tools, industry influencers)
- When they post questions or discussions, reply with helpful answer
- Don't pitch your product unless directly relevant
- Be the person who adds value, not the person who self-promotes

**Example:**
```
Someone tweets: "What's the best way to handle [technical problem]?"

Your reply (good):
"We ran into this last month. Tried [approach 1], but [issue].
Ended up using [approach 2] because [reason]. Worked well.
Happy to share code if helpful."

Your reply (bad):
"Use [your product]! It solves this perfectly. Link: [link]"
```

### LinkedIn Deep Dive

#### Algorithm Mechanics (2024-2025)

**What the algorithm rewards:**
1. **Dwell time**: How long people read your post (longer = better)
2. **Engagement from connections**: Comments from your direct network boost reach
3. **Shares outside LinkedIn**: If people share on email/Slack (tracked via link clicks)
4. **Profile keywords**: Posts about topics in your profile = shown to relevant audiences
5. **Document/carousel posts**: These get 2-3x more reach than text posts

**What the algorithm punishes:**
1. **External links in main post**: KILLS reach (put in first comment instead)
2. **Spammy behavior**: Tagging 10+ people, using bots, copy-paste comments
3. **Clickbait**: "You won't believe..." headlines (algorithm detects engagement bait)
4. **Low dwell time**: If people scroll past quickly, algorithm stops showing it

#### Content Formats That Perform

**1. The Carousel Post (Highest Engagement)**

**Why it works:**
- Visual format (stands out in feed)
- Multi-slide = high dwell time (algorithm loves this)
- Easy to skim (numbered insights)
- Highly shareable (people save and share carousels)

**Optimal structure:**
- **Slide 1**: Bold hook (question or controversial claim)
- **Slides 2-8**: Core content (insights, steps, examples)
- **Slide 9**: Summary + key takeaways
- **Slide 10**: CTA ("Follow [Your Name] for more [topic]")

**Design tips:**
- Consistent color scheme (brand recognition)
- Large, readable fonts (many read on mobile)
- One idea per slide (don't cram)
- Use templates (Canva has LinkedIn carousel templates)

**Example topic ideas:**
- "7 Mistakes We Made Scaling to $1M ARR"
- "The Framework We Use to Prioritize Features"
- "How We Reduced Churn from 8% to 2% in 90 Days"

---

**2. The Story Post (High Engagement)**

**Why it works:**
- Emotional resonance (people remember stories)
- Vulnerability builds trust
- Relatable (readers see themselves in your journey)

**Structure:**
```

"Three years ago, I was sitting in [place], staring at [detail].
We had just [failure/low point]."

Middle: [The journey]
"Here's what we tried:
- [Attempt 1 + why it failed]

- [Attempt 3 + the insight]"

Turning point: [The change]
"Then [specific event/realization] changed everything.
We realized [key insight]."

Outcome: [The result]
"Today: [current state with specific metrics/outcome]"

Lessons: [What you learned]
"If I could go back, here's what I'd tell myself:
1. [Lesson]
2. [Lesson]
3. [Lesson]"

CTA: [In first comment]
"If you're dealing with [problem], we built [product] to help: [link]"
```

#### Engagement Tactics

**Tactic 1: No Links in Main Post**

**Critical:** LinkedIn's algorithm HEAVILY penalizes external links in main post.

**Instead:**
- Write full post with no link
- Post it
- Immediately comment on your own post with link
- Pin your comment (so it stays at top)

**Example:**
```
Main post: [Full story/insights, no link]

Your first comment: "If you want to try [product]: [link]"
[Pin this comment]
```

---

**Tactic 2: Comment on Your Own Post**

**What:** After posting, add a comment with additional context or question.

**Why:**
- Kickstarts comment section (social proof)
- Gives you space for link (without hurting main post)
- Can add nuance or caveats

**Example:**
```
Main post: [Your content]

Your comment: "One thing I didn't mention above: [additional insight]

What's your experience with this? Have you found different approaches that work?"
```

---

**Tactic 3: Tag Thoughtfully (2-3 People Max)**

**What:** Tag people mentioned in post or who contributed to the work.

**Why:** They'll likely engage (comment/share), which signals to algorithm.

**How:**
- Only tag if genuinely relevant (not spammy)
- Thank teammates who contributed
- Credit sources for frameworks/ideas
- Max 2-3 tags (more looks desperate)

**Example:**
```
"None of this would've been possible without @[Teammate] who built the entire backend in 2 weeks."
```

---

**Tactic 4: Respond to Comments (Especially First Hour)**

**What:** Reply to every comment in first 1-2 hours.

**Why:** Algorithm sees "high engagement" and shows post to more people.

**How:**
- Substantive replies (not just "Thanks!")
- Ask follow-up questions (drives more replies)
- Add additional insights in replies (people read comment threads)

## Cross-Platform Repurposing

### Same Content, Different Formats

**Core idea:** Create once, repurpose for each platform.

#### Example: Feature Launch

**Twitter version (Thread):**
```
Just shipped [feature] 🚀

Here's why it matters:

[5-7 tweet thread with demo video]

Try it: [link in reply]
```

**LinkedIn version (Story post):**
```
We just launched [feature].

But let me tell you why we almost didn't build it.

[Story format: problem → resistance → insight → build → outcome]

[In first comment: link]
```

**Email version (Newsletter):**
```
Subject: We shipped [feature] (here's the story)

[Full story with screenshots, customer quotes, technical details]

[CTA: Try it here]
```

**Slack community version:**
```
Hey folks! We just shipped [feature].

Would love your feedback. Here's what it does: [demo link]

Any questions, drop them here and I'll answer.
```

---

### Batching Strategy

**Time investment:** 2-3 hours on Friday, content for the week.

**Process:**
1. **Review what shipped this week** (30 min)
   - What features/updates went live?
   - Any interesting bugs/challenges?
   - Customer feedback or notable wins?

2. **Pick 2-3 stories** (15 min)
   - Most interesting feature
   - Biggest challenge overcome
   - Surprising customer use case

3. **Create core content** (60 min)
   - Record 2-3 "vibe check" videos (5 min each)
   - Draft 1-2 threads (15 min each)
   - Write 1 long-form LinkedIn post (20 min)

4. **Repurpose** (30 min)
   - Turn thread into LinkedIn carousel (Canva template)
   - Pull quotes from video for image posts
   - Extract insights for newsletter snippet

5. **Schedule** (15 min)
   - Monday: Build-in-public update
   - Wednesday: Feature demo video
   - Friday: Lessons learned post
