# Viral Mechanics Reference

## Case Studies: Products That Won With Viral Artifacts

### Manus: The Invite Gate + Replay Strategy

**What they did:**
- Controlled access through invite system (scarcity + FOMO)
- Session replay feature lets users share agent task completions
- Every shared replay acts as a product demo to new audiences

**Why it worked:**
- Replay proves the product works (not a staged demo)
- Scarcity made getting access feel like winning
- Users gained status by showing off their "AI assistant at work"

**Key metrics:**
- Viral coefficient > 1.0 (each user brought >1 new user via replays)
- 40%+ of signups came from shared replay links
- Replay shares peaked when agent completed "impressive" tasks (multi-hour research condensed to 30 seconds)

**Lesson:** Observable labor (watching AI work) > static screenshots.

### Loom: Async Video Communication

**What they did:**
- Every video recorded gets a shareable link
- Video embeds work everywhere (Slack, email, Notion, etc.)
- Viewer doesn't need Loom account to watch

**Why it worked:**
- Every Loom video is a mini product demo
- "Watch this instead of reading a 10-paragraph email" = clear value prop
- Viral loop: Recipient sees video → "I need this tool" → signs up → creates videos → cycle repeats

**Key metrics:**
- 50M+ videos created (50M marketing assets in the wild)
- Majority of signups came from watching someone else's Loom
- Enterprise teams adopted organically (one user → whole team via shared videos)

**Lesson:** Make the core product output inherently shareable.

### Notion: Public Page + Template Gallery

**Key metrics:**
- Template gallery is #1 traffic source to Notion homepage

### Figma: Multiplayer Design + Public Files

**What they did:**
- Real-time collaboration (invite others to edit)
- Community files are public by default
- "Duplicate this file" = instant onboarding

**Why it worked:**
- Collaboration requires inviting teammates (built-in viral loop)
- Public files showcase what's possible (social proof + inspiration)

**Key metrics:**
- Average team size grew 3x year-over-year (collaboration drove expansion)
- 70%+ of signups came from being invited to a file
- Community files get millions of duplicates (each duplicate = new user activation)

**Lesson:** Collaboration as a core feature = built-in distribution.

### Calendly: Recipient Becomes User

**What they did:**

- "Get your own Calendly" CTA at bottom of booking page

**Why it worked:**
- Every meeting scheduled is a Calendly demo to the invitee

**Key metrics:**

## The Viral Coefficient Formula

**Viral Coefficient (K) = (# invites per user) × (conversion rate of invites)**

- **K < 1.0**: Growth requires paid acquisition
- **K = 1.0**: Self-sustaining growth (break-even)
- **K > 1.0**: Exponential viral growth

**Example calculations:**

| Product | Invites/User | Conversion | K | Outcome |
|---------|--------------|------------|---|---------|
| Dropbox Referral | 5 | 20% | 1.0 | Self-sustaining |
| Loom Video Share | 3 | 40% | 1.2 | Viral growth |
| Calendly Link | 10 | 15% | 1.5 | Strong viral growth |
| Figma Collaboration | 4 | 35% | 1.4 | Strong viral growth |

**How to increase K:**
1. Increase invites per user (make sharing easier/more valuable)
2. Increase conversion rate (reduce friction for invited users)

## Viral Artifact Taxonomy

### 1. Passive Artifacts (No User Action Required)

**Examples:**
- "Powered by [Product]" footer on generated content
- Email signatures with product branding
- Public URLs that include product name

**Pros:**
- Zero user effort (100% of users contribute)
- Scales automatically with usage

**Cons:**
- Lower conversion rates (viewers less engaged)
- Can feel spammy if too prominent

**Best for:** Infrastructure products, developer tools, content platforms

---

### 2. Semi-Active Artifacts (One-Click Sharing)

**Examples:**
- "Share this result" button that pre-fills social post
- Auto-generated weekly summary email (forward-friendly)
- Public profile pages (users just toggle public/private)

**Pros:**
- Higher engagement than passive (user opted in)
- Still low friction (one click vs multi-step)

**Cons:**
- Requires some user intent (lower volume than passive)

**Best for:** Productivity tools, analytics platforms, content creators

---

### 3. Active Artifacts (User-Created Content)

**Examples:**
- Templates shared to gallery
- Blog posts written about product experience
- Video tutorials created by power users

**Pros:**
- Highest quality (users invest time)
- Strongest social proof (authentic testimonials)

**Cons:**
- Lowest volume (only power users create)
- Requires community management

**Best for:** Complex tools with passionate user bases, developer platforms

## Psychological Triggers in Viral Artifacts

### Trigger 1: Social Proof

**Definition:** People do what they see others doing.

**How to use in artifacts:**
- Display view counts: "Watched by 10,247 people"
- Show user testimonials: "This saved me 10 hours this week"
- Feature top creators: "Creator of the month" badges

**Why it works:** Reduces perceived risk ("If 10K people tried this, it's probably safe/good").

---

### Trigger 2: Status Gain

**Definition:** People share things that make them look good.

**How to use in artifacts:**
- Highlight accomplishments: "You're in the top 5% of users"
- Make results impressive: "Built in 2 hours" (implies user skill)
- Provide bragging rights: "I automated my entire workflow"

**Why it works:** Sharing boosts sharer's perceived expertise/productivity.

---

### Trigger 3: FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)

**Definition:** People want what others have (especially if it's scarce).

**How to use in artifacts:**
- Invite-only access visible in artifacts: "Built with [Product] (invite-only)"
- Limited-time challenges: "Only 48 hours left to join"
- Exclusive features: "Early access to [new feature]"

**Why it works:** Scarcity increases perceived value.

---

### Trigger 4: Reciprocity

**Definition:** People feel obligated to give back when they receive.

**How to use in artifacts:**
- "You get X, they get Y" referral programs
- Template creators get attribution/traffic
- Collaboration loops (both parties benefit)

**Why it works:** Receiving value creates social debt (repaid by sharing/inviting).

---

### Trigger 5: Identity/Belonging

**Definition:** People adopt behaviors of their in-group.

**How to use in artifacts:**
- Community showcases: "Built by [job title/industry]"
- Tribe-specific features: "For [specific persona]"
- Shared challenges: "Join 1,000+ [identity] who completed this"

**Why it works:** Strengthens group identity (using product = being part of tribe).

## Platform-Specific Viral Mechanics

### Twitter/X

**Native viral mechanics:**
- Retweets (1-click amplification)
- Quote tweets (add commentary)
- Threads (serialized storytelling)

**Artifact optimization:**
- First tweet must hook (people scroll fast)
- Video auto-plays (grab attention in feed)
- Visual contrast (stand out in timeline)
- Tagged screenshots (show UI in action)

**Best artifact types:**
- 30-second demo videos
- Before/after comparisons
- "Here's what I built" threads
- Challenge results (leaderboard screenshots)

---

### LinkedIn

**Native viral mechanics:**
- Professional credibility (expertise signaling)
- Network notifications ("Your connection posted")
- Carousel posts (high engagement)

**Artifact optimization:**
- Professional framing (no memes, polished tone)
- ROI/time-saved metrics (business value)
- Lessons learned narrative (educational angle)
- Industry-specific use cases

**Best artifact types:**
- Case study carousels
- "How I achieved [result]" posts
- Tool stack breakdowns
- Professional milestones

---

### Reddit/Hacker News

**Native viral mechanics:**
- Upvotes (community curation)
- Comments (discussion drives visibility)
- "Show HN" / "Show Reddit" posts

**Artifact optimization:**
- Transparency (no marketing speak)
- Technical depth (show how it works)
- Open source/free tier (community-friendly)
- Authentic "just launched" narrative

**Best artifact types:**
- "Show HN: I built [thing]" posts
- Technical deep-dives
- Open-source project announcements
- Behind-the-scenes build logs

---

### Email/Slack

**Native viral mechanics:**
- Forwarding (personal recommendation)
- @mentions (notify teammates)
- Embeds (inline visibility)

**Artifact optimization:**
- Works without clicking links (inline preview)
- Clear value in subject line
- Mobile-friendly formatting
- One-click collaboration invite

**Best artifact types:**
- Weekly summaries (forward to team)
- Shared dashboards (embed in Slack)
- Template libraries (forward to colleagues)
- Achievement notifications (humble-brag to friends)

## Measuring Viral Artifact Success

### Primary Metrics

**1. Viral Coefficient (K)**
- Formula: (Artifacts shared per user) × (% of viewers who sign up)
- Target: K > 1.0 for self-sustaining growth
- Track: Weekly cohorts to see if K is improving

**2. Share Rate**
- Formula: (# artifacts shared) / (# artifacts generated)
- Target: >10% for passive artifacts, >30% for semi-active
- Track: By artifact type (which formats get shared most?)

**3. Conversion Rate**
- Formula: (# signups from artifact views) / (# artifact views)
- Target: >5% for warm traffic (shared by friend), >1% for cold (organic discovery)
- Track: By platform (Twitter vs LinkedIn vs email)

**4. Artifact Engagement**
- Formula: (Views + saves + shares) / (# artifacts)
- Target: Varies by platform (100+ for Twitter, 50+ for LinkedIn)
- Track: Which artifacts are "outliers" (study for patterns)

### Secondary Metrics

**5. Time-to-Share**
- How quickly after artifact generation do users share?
- Target: <5 minutes (still excited about accomplishment)

**6. Share Medium Distribution**
- Where are artifacts being shared? (Twitter, email, Slack, LinkedIn)
- Optimize for platforms with highest conversion

**7. Creator Activation Rate**
- % of users who make at least one shareable artifact
- Target: >50% (if lower, onboarding issue or lack of "impressive" use cases)

**8. Repeat Share Rate**
- % of users who share multiple artifacts (indicates ongoing value)
- Target: >20% (power users are your growth engine)

## Common Pitfalls & Solutions

### Pitfall 1: Artifacts Aren't Actually Impressive

**Symptom:** Low share rates even with easy sharing.

**Solution:** Audit what's being generated. Is the "observable labor" genuinely impressive? If your AI agent just sends one email, that's not share-worthy. If it reads 100 emails and auto-drafts responses, that is.

---

### Pitfall 2: Too Much Friction to Share

**Symptom:** High artifact generation but low share rate.

**Solution:** Reduce steps. Ideal: Zero-click (auto-shared to public gallery). Good: One-click (pre-filled social post). Bad: Multi-step (download, upload, format, post).

---

### Pitfall 3: No Creator Incentive

**Symptom:** Only 1-2% of users share.

**Solution:** What do sharers get? Add status (leaderboards), distribution (featured galleries), or rewards (credits for shares). If sharing doesn't benefit the user, they won't do it.

---

### Pitfall 4: Artifacts Don't Drive Conversions

**Symptom:** High views but low signups.

**Solution:** Is the product value clear in the artifact? Does the artifact link to a landing page? Is there a CTA? Artifacts must show value AND make signup easy.

---

### Pitfall 5: One-Hit Wonders

**Symptom:** One viral artifact, then growth flatlines.

**Solution:** Build systems, not campaigns. Every task completion should generate artifacts, not just one-off stunts. Virality compounds when it's built into product usage.

## Advanced Techniques

### Technique 1: Viral Artifact Stacking

Combine multiple artifact types to compound effects.

**Example:**
- AI app builder generates replay of build process (Artifact 1)
- Finished app added to public gallery (Artifact 2)
- User invited teammates to co-build (Artifact 3)
- Weekly email summarizes all apps built (Artifact 4)

Each artifact drives views to the others. Replay watchers browse gallery. Gallery browsers try building. Builders invite teammates.

---

### Technique 2: Cross-Platform Artifact Syndication

Automatically reformat artifacts for each platform.

**Example:**
- User completes task → artifact generated
- System creates:
  - 30-second video for Twitter
  - Carousel for LinkedIn
  - Embeddable widget for email
  - OG image for link shares
- User clicks one button, shares to all platforms simultaneously

---

### Technique 3: Artifact SEO

Design artifacts to rank in search engines.

**Example:**
- Each public creation gets its own URL
- URL structure: [product].com/[username]/[creation-name]
- Page includes: Full demo, creator bio, tech stack, related creations
- Long-tail keyword optimization: "How to build [specific thing]"

Result: Thousands of pages ranking for niche searches, each driving organic signups.

---

### Technique 4: Collaborative Artifact Challenges

Time-bound challenges that encourage group participation.

**Example:**
- Launch "Build a [thing] in 48 hours" challenge
- Public leaderboard shows all submissions
- Users invite friends to compete
- Top submissions get featured (status incentive)

Result: Spike in artifacts generated, shared, and viewed (concentrated viral effect).

## Conclusion

Viral artifacts turn product usage into marketing fuel.

The best artifacts are:
- **Auto-generated** (no user effort required)
- **Impressive** (observable labor worth sharing)
- **Platform-optimized** (native format for each channel)
- **Creator-incentivized** (status/distribution for sharing)
- **Conversion-optimized** (clear path from view → signup)

Build once, benefit forever. Every new user generates more artifacts, which attract more users.

That's the compounding power of product-shaped marketing.
